Introduction
Rice is a key dietary crop for major Asian countries, including India, China, Bangladesh and as well
as many parts of Africa. As per the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO) prediction, production
of rice will be essential to rise by 25–30% to achieve global requirement by 2050.China being the
world’s largest rice producer heavily relies on rice production which has also faced major decline in
agriculture sectors for rice due to labor shortages, limited cultivable land, and growing concerns
due to climate variability. The world's second-largest producer of farming products i.e India, has
also witnessed its agriculture sector's GDP share decline from 57% in 1950–51 to 18.3% in 2023
owing to systemic problems like policy ineffectiveness, limiting utilization of technology, and
infrastructure gaps, despite its continuing reliance on major crops like rice and wheat. Rice
production is extremely susceptible to a variety of bacterial, viral, and fungal diseases are, Brown
Spot, Bacterial Leaf Blight, Downy Mildew, Bacterial Leaf Streak, Bacterial Panicle Blight, Blast, Dead
Heart, Hispa, Normal, Tungro under different environmental condition. Figure 1. Images of 9
different diseases on rice leaves. The FAO stated that the diseases on crops can lower agricultural
production by 10–30% each year, with major outbreaks in rice fields resulting in yield reductions of

are significantly impacted by early-stage lesions, which frequently start out as tiny, asymmetrical

in farmer experience, environmental illumination conditions, and disease similarities between
classes, this technique is labor-intensive, particular, and susceptible to faults. As a result,
inappropriate pesticide use, higher production costs, and substantial financial loss are frequently
caused by delayed or inaccurate detection.

Several researches are conducted to increase the production of rice using Artificial Intelligence,
Internet of things and embedded systems. Disease patch detection before time on crops is very
crucial for the rice manufacturers / rice farmers. AI and IOT together hand-in-hand have shown that
disease detection can be fast forwarded by farmers relying on the remote-edge-computing systems
which have AI embedded in them. Recent breakthroughs in deep learning have resulted in
convolutional neural network (CNN) models which automates the detection of plant disease. The
major approaches only focus on the classification of disease occurrence rather than accurate lesion
localization [1], however CNN classifiers have revealed great image-level precision. Though, exact
lesion limit detection is required for applied ground deployment, principally when numerous
diseases co-occur on the similar leaf external. Applications of agriculture are explored for object
detection models like YOLOv8 and Faster R-CNN; however, all the specified models are mainly
intended for general object detection tasks. Smallest lesion sizes, irregular forms, lapping diseased
regions, and complex background interference are features of small-grained, texture-driven
patterns of disease that they recurrently struggle with. In spite of substantial development, some
methodological gaps exist. First, texture-aware feature extraction algorithms tailored to plant
disease are absent from current detection frameworks. Second, insufficient multiple scales
representation and anchor modification techniques continue to result in subpar small-lesion
detection performance [1][2]. Third, CNN–architectures that integrate micro feature extraction

alongside global contextual modeling—a crucial prerequisite for capturing both macro-level leaf
structural interactions and micro-level lesion details—are not well integrated [3]. Lastly,
agricultural datasets, which show substantial intra-class resemblance and inter-class variation
under uncontrolled climatic conditions, are not specifically catered for by many existing models.
[3][4] The creation of a highly accurate and comprehensive framework for the localization of rice
diseases is hampered by these constraints taken together.
This study suggests OryzaYOLOCNN-X, a texture-aware, multi-scale object recognition framework
created especially for fine-grained rice leaf disease localization, as a solution to these problems. For
improved texture representation, the suggested architecture combines neural feature extraction
with dilated receptive fields. A hybrid SIoU–Focal loss formulation improves bounding box
regression accuracy and reduces class imbalance effects, while an adaptive anchor clustering
technique is used to improve localization of both small and large lesion patches. The suggested
system seeks to greatly increase multi-scale detection robustness and precision under challenging
agricultural imaging settings through this unified architecture.
Literature Review
Research on rice disease categorization is dominated by CNN architectures. Rajareddy et al. [1]
developed a digital twin (DT) that assisted IoAT design in order to identify and categorize Oryzasativa
diseases. The study improved the architecture of AlexNet with Quaternion convolution layers—
encoding color info as four-dimension vectors to capture phase as well direction patterns—and
Atrous’ (dilated) convolution layers in order that enlarged the receptive field without raising the
parameter count, which enabled detection of subtle disease pat CNN hyperparameters were
optimized using a Chaotic Honey Badger Algorithm (CHBA). An average precision of 93.5% was
achieved by the model. The Fog-Edge paradigm performs better than cloud-only implementation
for real-time agriculture disease control, according to service latency study. [6] Chakrabarty et al.
formed a hybrid model which combines a Bidirectional Encoder Representations from
Transformers for Images (BEiT) by pre-trained CNNs to recognize rice leaf disease. The classification
of Brown Spot and Rice Blast, the model performed to well compared to prior transformer-based
and other deep learning models with a accuracy of 0.97, remembrance of 0.96, and F1-score of
0.97. They also used visual segmentation and Local interpreting Model-Agnostic Explanations
(LIME) to improve model transparency. [7] Ali et al. developed an ensemble strategy that combines
DenseNet201, EfficientNetB0, InceptionResNetV2, and EfficientNetB3 to advance plant disease
Categorization accuracy. They solved class imbalance by employing a unique image processing
technology and a data-balancing mechanism. When tested on the New Plant Village database with
38 disease types, the ensemble obtained 99.89% accuracy and showed excellent generalizability.
[8] Aggarwal et al. developed a data-effective amalgamated learning system for disease
classification of rice leaf’s based on EfficientNetB3. This approach, which combines federated
transfer learning with extraction of features, achieves 99% accuracy for validation while using less
GPU memory and processing power than centralized methods. By dispersing training models across

3.5 Hybrid YOLO11 and Custom CNN Architecture
Stage 1: YOLO11 based patch detection
In this stage of the hybrid model, a YOLO11 based object i.e patch detection model is applied to scrap
the patches of images where disease is localized, thus fingerprint of each image is obtained in this
stage. This step is basically a preprocessing step used to remove other part from image which does
not hold importance in disease identification, basically noise reduction is attained in this stage.
The dataset from IEEE-Dataport [57] is prepared using LabelImg to form bounding boxes required for
training and testing in the YOLO11 model. The bounding boxes provided through LabelImg served
the candidate patches that contained disease features like texture variations, lesions and
discoloration.
The images from dataset were resized into pixels box of size 512*512 for improving the
performance of the algorithm for detecting the patches in a generalized way. Post detection, the
identified patches were cropped from the actual image. These extracted patches which served as
generalized candidates for the disease type were passed to the next stage of the architecture for
further classification.
Stage 2: Custom CNN for classification of disease type
In this stage, a convolutional Neural Network which is custom made is utilized for carrying out the
rice disease type classification on the dataset of regions/patches extracted from the previous
stage. This customized CNN was designed to detect the spatial attention and texture patterns
across the patches, which were identified as subtle variations in leaf diseases.

Discussion
The proposed hybrid YOLO-CNN architecture outperforms single-stage models because of the
complementing capabilities of its individual component.
1. Complementary Features Learning
In the first stage, disease-affected regions are spatially localized in complex field situations using
YOLO-based object detection. This is especially crucial for airborne or in-field imaging, where noise
is greatly increased by background features like dirt, water, and overlapping foliage. YOLO lowers
background interference by separating pertinent areas. The Custom CNN then works on these
limited areas to extract fine-grained information such as lesion morphology, color gradients, and
texture differences. Accurate disease identification hinge on the model's capability to extract both
local discriminatory forms and comprehensive spatial background, which is made conceivable by its
two-stage strategy.


                                                                                                     948
Journal of Intelligent Decision Making and Information Science
Volume 3(7s), (2026) 933-956



2. Reduction of False Positives
In assessment of the end-to-end detection model, the hybrid pipeline intensely declines false
positive estimates. By eliminating needless parts of the image, the YOLO step functions as a area
filter, giving the CNN classifier clear inputs. This leads to improved features learning and
classification precision. In visually jumbled settings, where single-stage models often misclassify
background artifacts as disease sign, experimental observations show a discernible decrease in
false positives.
3. Confusion Matric Analysis
Confusion matrix study is light on class-specific occurrences and model performance.
Pairs of Diseases which were challenging were Bacterial Leaf Blight & Bacterial Panicle Blight are
specifically confusing, with considerable misclassification marks by models. Possible reasons are:
• Lesion patterns are strikingly similar in early-stage symptoms.
• Similar coloring and structural damage to leaf tissues are caused by both diseases.
• Separability is further diminished by variations in illumination and picture collecting conditions.
From the standpoint of plant pathology, these diseases are intrinsically challenging to differentiate
using RGB imagery alone since they share overlapping symptom presentations during the early
stages of infection.
4. Strategies for Mitigation
The succeeding strategies to deal with these issues are as follows:
• Temporal Monitoring: Monitoring the course of an illness throughout time to identify
variations according to stages.
• Multi-spectral imaging uses spectral bands other than RGB to show physiological changes that
are invisible to the human eye.
• Increasing early-stage infection samples to enhance model learning is known as data
enrichment.
5. Strengths of the Model
With approximately 97% classification precision, the proposed model achieves remarkably good on
illnesses including Bacterial Leaf Streak, Hispa, and Dead Heart. Reasons are as follow:
• Unmistakable visual indicators, such as Hispa's and Dead Heart's structural distortion
• Sufficient training examples to guarantee strong feature learning
• Unambiguous inter-class separability and intra-class consistency
These findings show that the model successfully captures biologically significant traits that are in line
with domain-specific illness characteristics.

Limitations and future Scope
Limitations:
1. Diversity of Datasets
The proposed model's capacity to oversimplify across many farmers may be obstructed by the
dataset it was trained on, which only included a minor amount of rice varieties. Classification
performance may be obstructed by alterations in plant morphology, disease expression across
various rice genotypes and texture. Furthermore, the dataset's limited geographic diversity may
limit its resilience to changing agroclimatic conditions.
2. Coverage of Diseases
The proposed framework focusses on nine primary rice diseases, while numerous minor diseases
and nutrient shortages are not addressed. Furthermore, accurate classification is misclassified by
the model's failing to explicitly handle varied diseases, which occur when various diseases coexist
on a single leaf. Despite the model's complete decent performance, early-stage disease finding is
still moderately less accurate because of pale and misty visual
3. Validation of Ground Truth
There may be some tagging ambiguity because the dataset be subject to expert annotations. Thus,
no laboratory-based or molecular investigative validation (such as PCR-based confirmation) was
supported out to authenticate the presence of the disease.
Future Scope:
Future scope will include multi modal fusion for different RGB photography through UAV for
validation. Future validation will also have multi-season and multi-location farm testing to
authenticate the strength under a different environmental situation. Participatory study which will
be involving producers who can provide useful data about effectiveness and useableness.
The real-time deployment of the proposed system utilizing UAV platforms for automatic farm
observation is a significant future goal. High-resolution inflight photos can be acquired by the UAV-
mounted imagery sensors and examined either onboard (Embedded raspberry Pi) or by a cloud-
edge hybrid system. This leads to:
• Quick scanning of vast agricultural regions
• Geotagging and real-time illness detection
• Making maps of the geographical distribution of diseases
• Combining precision spraying systems with targeted intervention
By linking and filling the gap between the agricultural application and lab research, such UAV-
enabled deployment makes crop health monitoring scalable and effective.

Conclusions
This study created and validated a hybrid YOLO-CNN framework to perform real-time rice disease
diagnosis, addressing the important requirement for automated, early stages disease monitoring in
precision farming systems.
Major Accomplishments:
1. Performance of the Model
The proposed approach achieved well on both real-world and standard datasets. While standard
models like ResNet50 and EfficientNet attained 99% and 98% accuracy, respectively, the Custom
CNN only achieved 96%. The proposed model achieved a far better trade-off amongst
computational and performance requirement, even though its precision is marginally worse. The
system showed strong generalization capabilities when it was further verified on datasets
representative of the field.
2. Practical Deployment



                                                                                                 951
Journal of Intelligent Decision Making and Information Science
Volume 3(7s), (2026) 933-956



The model was utilized effectively on edge-based hardware, using a Raspberry Pi 4B model, with an
inference speed of around 18.5 FPS and a latency of 52 ms. Deployment on devices with limited
resources is ensured by the lightweight architecture (3.56 MB model size). Real-time disease
identification during field surveillance operations is made possible by these features, which
facilitate seamless integration with AV (Aerial Vehicle)-based systems.
3. Agricultural Impact
The proposed method detects illnesses in their initial to middle stages, which is vital for timely
management and avoiding yield loss. The technology allows for scalable monitoring of roughly 20–
30 hectares each day per UAV and lessens reliance on professional agronomists for routine scouting
by automating disease monitoring. This enhances precision agriculture decision-making, lowers
input costs, and improves crop health management.
4. Methodological Advancements
Several significant contributions are made by this work:
• Demonstrates the efficiency of a hybrid detection-classification process that combines spatial
localization and deep feature extraction.
• Provides an attention-based CNN architecture and a multi-scale architecture, for improving
detection discrimination.
• Model performance is validated using both standard benchmark and field representative
datasets
• Cross Regional Validation also applied and achieved a benchmark accuracy.
