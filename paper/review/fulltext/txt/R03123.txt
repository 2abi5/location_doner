                                                                                              fuses high-dimensional image features, extracted using the DinoV3 encoder, with numerical count

                                                                                              temporal dependencies and handles irregular sampling intervals common in greenhouse monitoring.

2.4. Uncertainty quantification
     Due to its significance, there have been multiple at-
tempts to integrate uncertainty quantification techniques into
the agricultural machine learning framework. Wang et al.
(2019) use a full Bayesian approach to estimate uncertainty
for subsoil heterogeneity estimations. They specify a prior
distribution and use Bayes’ rule to get the posterior as
they use a model with a relatively low number of param-
eters compared to deep learning models. MC-Dropout and
Stochastic Gradient Langevin Dynamics (SGLD) Welling
and Teh (2011) have been used by Hernández and López
(2020) as an approximation of a full Bayesian approach for
plant disease detection. Chrispell et al. (2021) and Meenken
et al. (2021) have investigated Bayesian approaches for real-
time crop management and estimating crop sensitivity to
weather changes, using hybrid modeling that combines ma-
chine learning with mechanistic models.
     Several works Chrispell et al. (2021); Padarian et al.
(2022); Li et al. (2023); Celikkan et al. (2023) have fo-
cused on using MC-Dropout or ensembles to estimate data
uncertainty or model uncertainty in deep learning models
for applications in agricultural or soil modeling. These ap-
proaches are computationally efficient during training and
provide a direct way to measure uncertainty estimates by
using metrics such as mean and variance. As an alternative,      Figure 1: Glasshouse at the University of Bonn Campus Klein
