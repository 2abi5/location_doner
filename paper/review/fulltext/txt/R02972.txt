ARTICLE INFO                                         Abstract
Keywords:                                            Image-based plant-disease classifiers often achieve high accuracy under controlled conditions, but
Strawberry disease                                   their reliability can deteriorate in real-world field environments. This article investigates cross-
Cross-dataset evaluation                             dataset strawberry leaf-health classification using controlled PlantVillage images and field images
Machine learning                                     collected through the SmartBerry project in Nigeria. Four deep-learning architectures were evaluated
Deep learning                                        under within-domain, controlled-to-field and pooled-training conditions using healthy-leaf and dark-
Semi-supervised learning                             spot/scorch symptoms. Although all models achieved near-ceiling performance on PlantVillage,
MobileNetV3-Small                                    direct transfer to SmartBerry reduced macro F1-score to between 0.418 and 0.824, demonstrating
MobileViTv2-0.5                                      a substantial gap between controlled-dataset performance and field reliability. To reduce the amount
EfficientNet-B0                                      of locally labelled field data required to address this gap, this paper proposes SmartBerry Domain
ConvNeXt-Tiny                                        Adaptation (SmartBerry-DA). This label-efficient adaptation approach combines a model trained
Smart agriculture                                    on controlled images with a small set of labelled SmartBerry images and additional unlabelled
                                                     field images. Using MobileViTv2-0.5, SmartBerry-DA achieved a macro F1-score of 0.940 ± 0.015
                                                     with only five labelled SmartBerry images per class and 0.988 ± 0.012 with ten, compared with
                                                     0.994 ± 0.008 when the full labelled target dataset was used. This shows that strong field performance
                                                     can be recovered with substantially reduced local annotation. For lightweight offline deployment, 16-
                                                     bit floating-point representation reduced model size from 4.37 MB to 2.30 MB without changing
                                                     predictive performance under the evaluated conditions. The findings demonstrate the importance
                                                     of locally acquired field data and label-efficient adaptation for transferring agricultural AI from
                                                     benchmark datasets to practical farming environments, while providing a pathway towards lightweight
                                                     strawberry disease recognition in settings where extensive annotation and continuous connectivity
                                                     may not be feasible.
