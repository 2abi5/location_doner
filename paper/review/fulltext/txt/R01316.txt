Machine learning approaches for wheat yield prediction integrating
biophysical modeling and remote sensing: Effects of sample size,
dimensionality, and transferability

A R T I C L E I N F O                                      A B S T R A C T

Keywords:                                                  Prediction of wheat yield plays a crucial role in mitigating the impacts of climate extremes and ensuring food
Machine learning techniques                                security. While vegetation indices (VIs) derived from remote sensing have been widely used for this purpose,
Remote sensing                                             integrating biophysical variables, such as crop stress, simulated using a biophysical model has the potential to
Biophysical variable
                                                           enhance model robustness. However, the role of integrating biophysical stress information in machine learning
Sample size
                                                           based yield prediction, particularly in terms of sample size efficiency, dimensionality reduction, and spatial
Dimensionality
Transferability                                            transferability, remains insufficiently examined. In this study, we address this gap by systematically evaluating
                                                           the impact of incorporating a Stress Index (SI) using three widely applied machine learning (ML) models Random
                                                           Forest (RF), Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), and Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) to predict wheat yield
                                                           across five Australian states. These models leveraged both Sentinel-2-derived VIs at the peak of greenness and a
                                                           biophysical factor: the SI.
                                                              The integration of the SI led to clear improvements in model performance. For XGBoost, the coefficient of
                                                           determination (R²) increased from 0.65 to 0.73, and the RMSE decreased from 0.67 to 0.58. GPR showed an
                                                           increase in R² from 0.66 to 0.70 and a reduction in RMSE from 0.76 to 0.60. RF reached an R² of 0.68 and an
                                                           RMSE of 0.64 after including SI. Additionally, SI contributed to dimensionality reduction, lowering the number
                                                           of required VIs from 11 to 6 without loss of accuracy. The inclusion of SI also improved spatial transferability.
                                                           When tested in Western Australia, cross-regional RMSE decreased by 0.09 for XGBoost, 0.16 for GPR, and 0.13
                                                           for RF. These results highlight that integrating biophysical crop responses, such as SI, into ML model calibration
                                                           can enhance the reliability and robustness of yield prediction using remote sensing data.
