  TLCp-SNN: A Dual-Branch Framework Integrating Transfer Learning-based
  Feature Selection with Spiking Neural Networks for Robust Mixed Crop Weed
                                  Classification

                                           Gulshan Kumar1*                    Chandani Sharma1

                 1
                  Maharishi Markandeshwar Institute of Computer Technology & Business Management,
                                      Mullana-Ambala, Haryana, 133207, India
                           * Corresponding author’s Email: gulshankumar07001@gmail.com


  Abstract: A challenging and important activity in precision agriculture is weed classification. Although deep learning
  procedures succeed, current single-branch feature extraction networks are unable to demonstrate complementary
  modalities of representation that are needed to distinguish morphologically similar types of weeds. The current paper
  proposes a dual-branch system, TLCp-SNN, which combines Transfer Learning-based Mallows Cp (TLCp) feature
  selection with Spiking Neural Network (SNN) temporal encoding to achieve strong multiclass classification of weeds.
  The TLCp branch takes advantage of cross-domain knowledge transfer to yield statistically optimal discriminative
  features selection. The SNN branch uses Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neurons with two hidden layers (256 and 128
  units) that are trained with Adam optimiser and triangular surrogate gradient to adapt static features into time-varying
  spike patterns.The final fused representation is 412-dimensional (d1 = 268 from TLCp, d2 = 144 from SNN; 268 +
  144 = 412), classified by an Extra Trees ensemble. Domain shift is quantified through proxy A-distance on all source–
  target pairs, confirming TLCp gains are largest when dA < 1.2. All baselines are reproduced under the same splits,
  augmentation, and evaluation pipeline; comparison tables are provided for all three datasets. DeepWeeds (17,509
  images, 9 classes), Plant Seedlings (5,539 images, 12 classes), and CottonWeedDet12 (5,648 images, 12 classes)
  experiments show competitive classification accuracy of 98.73%, 99.21, and 97.86, respectively. Experiments of
  ablation validate synergistic enhancement of 3.91% over the conservative baseline and 5-fold cross-validation with
  Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (p < 0.05) prove the statistical soundness.
  Keywords: Weed classification, Deep learning, Transfer learning, Feature selection, Spiking neural networks,
  Mallows’ Cp, Precision agriculture, Domain adaptation.
